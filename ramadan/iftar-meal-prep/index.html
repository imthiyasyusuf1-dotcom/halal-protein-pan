Cook once, eat for four days

Cooking every afternoon while fasting is hard work. This plan cooks once every four days instead. Each cook day makes two mains (call them A and B) and one suhoor batch. Every recipe makes four servings, so that is eight portions of main and four suhoors from one session in the kitchen.

  • Iftar alternates: main A, main B, main A, main B.
  • The late meal, after taraweeh, is half a portion of the other main.
  • Suhoor is one portion of the batch each morning.
  • The freezer gets the day 4 boxes plus one spare portion of each main, so nothing sits in the fridge for more than three days.

Seven cook days take you to day 28. Days 29 and 30 run off the spare portions in your freezer, with a half batch of oats made on day 28. That last stretch, when the nights are busiest, needs no cooking at all.

Opening the fast, then the main

Open your fast with dates and water as you normally would, pray Maghrib, then sit down to your main. A reheated box is ready in about five minutes, which means you are eating with the family rather than standing at the hob. Add rice or a chapati, a salad or a bowl of raita on the side.

Keep samosas and pakoras as a treat rather than the main event. A plate of them can cost as many calories as the whole main, with a fraction of the protein.

Cook day rules that keep it simple

  • Fry a big batch of onions before Ramadan and freeze it in portions. It takes 20 minutes off every cook day.
  • Freeze rice dishes in portions on the day you cook them. Cool rice within 1 hour and keep it in the fridge for no more than 1 day.
  • Reheat food once only, until steaming hot all the way through.
  • Label every box with the day number, not the recipe name. At 4am and at sunset you only need to know which box is next.
  • For Eid, double any curry. The macros per serving stay the same.
